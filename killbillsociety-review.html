Killbill Society (2025)

Director: Srijit Mukherji Year: 2025 Genre: Thriller, Drama
2.5 / 5.0

A Dark Comedy That Struggles to Find Its Footing

Srijit Mukherji's Killbill Society, the sequel to Hemlock Society, attempts to tackle the heavy subject of suicide awareness with dark humor and clever writing. While the film has moments of brilliance, it ultimately falls short of its predecessor's impact, feeling repetitive and overly niche in its appeal. The premise—centered around Ananda Kar now Mrityunjay Kar, who runs Killbill Society, an Organization that helps people to die who are afraid of suicide — holds promise, but the execution lacks the freshness and boldness that made the original stand out.

Uneven Performances and Missed Opportunities

Koushani Mukherjee shoulders the lead role, but her performance, while earnest, lacks the depth needed to fully engage the audience. Parambrata Chattopadhyay, however, delivers a standout performance, particularly in the film's climactic scenes where his emotional range shines. The supporting cast, including Biswanath Basu, Anindya Chatterjee, Tulika Basu and Sandipta Sen, do their best with limited material, but their characters often feel underdeveloped, leaving their potential untapped.

The film's pacing is another stumbling block. While Hemlock Society was sharp and impactful, Killbill Society drags in places, with jokes that start to feel recycled and a screenplay that occasionally veers into laziness. The humor, though witty in spots, doesn't sustain its edge throughout the runtime.

Bright Spots in an Otherwise Mixed Bag

Despite its flaws, the film isn't without its merits. The soundtrack is a highlight, offering refreshing and soothing tracks that elevate certain scenes. The opening credits sequence is another standout—creative and playful, with the entire crew making quirky appearances as their names flash on screen. These moments provide a glimpse of the creativity that could have elevated the entire film.

Visually, the film maintains a polished look, with decent cinematography and production design that suit its dark comedic tone. However, these technical strengths aren't enough to compensate for the narrative and pacing issues.

The Verdict

Killbill Society is a mixed bag—a film with flashes of brilliance but hampered by uneven pacing, repetitive humor, and underdeveloped characters. While Parambrata's performance and the soundtrack make it worth a one-time watch, it doesn't quite live up to the hype or the legacy of its predecessor. Fans of Srijit Mukherji's work might find moments to appreciate, but the film ultimately feels like a missed opportunity.
